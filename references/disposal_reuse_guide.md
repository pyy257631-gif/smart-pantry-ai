# Expired-Food Handling & Reuse Guide

> Principle: **safety first; when in doubt, throw it out.** Reuse ideas only apply to food that is unspoiled and low risk.

## 1. Three-step edibility check

1. **Category risk**: meat, poultry, seafood, eggs, cooked food, soy products, cut fruit and dairy are high risk after expiry; dry sealed shelf-stable goods are lower risk.
2. **Sensory check**: look at color/texture, smell, and (only for low-risk items) taste a tiny amount. Stop at any abnormality.
3. **Storage history**: was it continuously refrigerated/frozen, within date, and not repeatedly thawed? Broken cold chain raises risk even before the date.

## 2. Hard red lines — discard entirely

Do **not** cut off a small part and keep eating when any of these appear:

- Mold, mold spots or a fuzzy film (toxins and hyphae can spread beyond the visible area).
- **Swollen** pouch, can or lid (botulism risk).
- Off smell, rot, abnormal sour/alcoholic smell (for non-fermented food).
- Sliminess, stringy texture, unusual bubbles or strong discoloration.
- Rusted, damaged, seam-dented or leaking cans with failed seals.
- Expired infant food, meat, seafood or prepared deli food.
- Rancid ("paint-like"/oxidized) smell in nuts, cooking oil or snacks — oxidation products are harmful.

Exception: for dense hard cheese or firm vegetables (e.g. hard cheese, carrot), a small mold spot may be removed by cutting at least **2 cm around and below** it; soft/porous foods (bread, cake, berries, meat) should be discarded whole once moldy.

## 3. Reuse ideas (unspoiled, low-risk only)

### Dairy
- **Milk just past date, no off smell**: use in dough/steamed buns/baking; diluted as a skin soak (patch-test first); polish leather goods; lightly wipe plant leaves; diluted and fermented as plant fertilizer.
- **Yogurt**: baking, face mask (if tolerated), or cooking if still fresh.
- **Butter**: leather care, hinge lubrication.

### Staples & bakery
- **Bread**: dry into breadcrumbs/croutons, bread pudding; small amounts for birds.
- **Cooked rice (unspoiled)**: congee/rice paste; stale rice as a cleaning paste.
- **Flour/rice with insects**: clean and inspect; use for cleaning or fully composted fertilizer rather than eating.

### Drinks & condiments
- **Beer**: hair rinse, wipe plant leaves, cleaning/deodorizing soak.
- **Coffee grounds**: deodorizer, degreasing scrub, or compost for acid-loving plants.
- **Tea leaves**: deodorizer, dried pillow filling, compost; use for tea eggs only if still fresh.
- **Honey**: naturally shelf-stable; crystallization is normal; use in masks or as a sugar substitute.
- **Flat juice**: not for drinking; can be fermented into cleaning enzyme.
- **Rancid cooking oil**: never eat; use for handmade soap or machinery lubrication.

### Produce
- **Wilted vegetables**: compost or cleaning enzyme (not for drinking).
- **Citrus peel**: deodorizer, limescale wiping, infused cleaner.
- **Rotten fruit**: compost or discard if moldy.

### Eggs
- **Spread-yolk but odorless egg**: not recommended to eat; can be buried/crushed as fertilizer away from roots.

## 4. Waste sorting

Follow **local** regulations. A common Chinese-city baseline:

| Stream | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Food waste (wet/kitchen) | leftovers, leaves, peels, food itself | remove plastic/metal packaging first |
| Residual (dry/other) waste | large bones, shells, cling film, food packaging | large bones often not accepted as kitchen waste |
| Recyclables | glass bottles, metal cans, clean plastic boxes | empty and lightly rinse |
| Hazardous waste | rarely food-related | follow local rules |

## 5. Disclaimer

- General household guidance, **not professional food-safety or medical advice**; eating expired food can cause foodborne illness.
- Infants, pregnant people, older adults, patients and immunocompromised users should eat only fresh, in-date, correctly stored food.
- Skin/cleaning reuse varies by individual; patch-test before skin contact.
- If unsure, discard it.
